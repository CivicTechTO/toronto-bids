## Interpretation and limits

Compass’s $3 million is a contribution from the vendor to the Zoo for facilities/equipment, not a payment from the Zoo to the vendor. The advertising reports seek annual creative reimbursement and media spending caps rather than establish new competitive award prices. Their cached May 4 / January 29 dates are staff report dates, not independently verified approval dates. The existing TRCA-only date correction rule must not be silently applied to Zoo records. No Zoo date correction is needed for the two NULL-date identity recoveries.

The capital status sources distinguish construction already awarded, unnamed submissions under evaluation, prequalified tendering, future proposed awards, and two RFP rounds with no submissions. A model supplier called “Not stated” is a placeholder, not an identified bidder or a named firm declining to bid. Budget/cash flow values are not bid or award prices. Generator installation and generator purchase are separate procurements.

The shared-services report concerns City of Toronto consulting assignments; a Zoo packet containing it does not establish the Zoo as buyer of those assignments. The governance references identify Board/Council policy reports, not procurement calls. No underlying public assignment call was proven for these records.

Direct TMMIS decision-page requests were inaccessible (403), and guessed dated exports were absent (404). No unread minutes are claimed. A 2020 master-plan document containing stale 2015 submission-label text was rejected as an identity bridge. The remaining holds may be resolved by future exact primary-source relationships; this review does not declare them globally unresolvable.

All first-party quotations in the JSON are bound to the file that actually contains them, with its URL and full SHA. The original procurement reports’ recommendations and the held status report’s historical approval confirmation remain distinct.
